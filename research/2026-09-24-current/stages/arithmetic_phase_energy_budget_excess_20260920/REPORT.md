# An explicit phase-energy law, and the limit of two energy budgets

Both incoming batches authenticate and replay unchanged. The continuation

## New arithmetic result

For the actual rough source on both original windows, and every fixed
smooth profile, the limiting phase-weighted quadratic energy has only
three nonzero Fourier coefficients (and their conjugates):

    g0 = 15/4,
    g1 = gamma in (0.03529691049240, 0.03529691049241),
    g2 = -1/108.

All fixed coefficients beyond order two vanish in this normalized limit.
The source-profile factor is D(x)=(4/15) integral_0^1 c_x(y)^2 dy.
This follows from the inherited ratio-Gram theorem, roughness at 2,3,5,
and a new nonstationary-carrier argument, not assumed independence.

For a unit numerator direction xi, the limiting adverse-phase mass is

    m_inf(xi)=15/(4*pi)-(gamma/2)Re(xi)-Re(xi^2)/(162*pi)>1.17.

The gated quadratic energy divided by d tends to D(x)m_inf(xi), uniformly
on a fixed compact profile and over all directions. The finite-cutoff
argument gives no effective rate or accuracy for a growing harmonic array.

## What this reveals about the proposed payment

The actual nonlinear adverse response tends uniformly to zero, by the
already established first-moment law. But even the ideal two-budget
envelope, supplied with EXACT total and adverse-phase energies, retains
an eventual strictly positive normalized excess on each fixed compact.

The reason is explicit: a constant amplitude allocation can satisfy both
energy budgets and produce a nonvanishing relaxed response. The actual
source instead concentrates quadratic energy in rare large values. The
energy-only relaxation cannot distinguish these behaviours.

This is not a failure of the two incoming derivations or their finite
certificates. Nor does it show that every directional certificate fails.
It shows that improving numerical precision in these same two budgets
cannot, by itself, make their normalized overpayment vanish.
