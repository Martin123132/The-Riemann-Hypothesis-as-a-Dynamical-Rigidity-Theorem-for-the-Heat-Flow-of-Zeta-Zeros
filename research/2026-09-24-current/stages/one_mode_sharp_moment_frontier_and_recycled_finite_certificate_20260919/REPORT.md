## 1. The cone bound is sharp given its three moments

With E=integral r^2, Q=integral z^2, M=integral rz, |z|<=r, the largest
possible J=integral z_+^2 consistent with these moments is exactly

    U(E,Q,M)=Q                              when M>=Q,
              (QE-M^2)/(Q+E-2M)            when M<Q.

Explicit two-point laws attain both branches. Thus no stronger universally
valid upper bound using ONLY E,Q,M can improve this envelope. Actual RH
arithmetic may impose additional restrictions not captured by those three
moments. The theorem is not a statement that the actual source is extremal.

After the audited compact-first collapse Q=E/2+o(log N), and writing e,m
for common normalized limiting moments, the exact condition for this moment
certificate to fit below c=0.04078 is:

* e<2c: the unshifted square already suffices;
* e=2c: require m<c;
* e>2c: require m<c-sqrt((e/2-c)(e-c)).

This replaces a guessed parameter by a precise joint arithmetic target.
It does not prove that the target holds.
